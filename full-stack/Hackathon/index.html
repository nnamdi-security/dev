The Semantic HTML & Accessibility

Semantic HTML refers to writing HTML in a way that clearly describes the meaning, purpose, and structure of the content on a webpage rather than focusing only on appearance or layout.

In semantic HTML, elements are chosen based on what the content represents. This creates a webpage structure that is meaningful not only to developers, but also to browsers, search engines, assistive technologies, and other systems that interpret web content.

Semantic HTML improves the organization and readability of code because each section of a webpage has a clearly defined role and purpose. It helps developers understand the structure of a website more easily, especially when working in teams or maintaining projects over time.

Accessibility in web development is the practice of designing and building websites, applications, and digital content so that all people, including individuals with disabilities, can use and interact with them effectively.

The goal of accessibility is to remove barriers that may prevent users from accessing information or performing tasks on a website. Accessibility considers users with different types of impairments, including visual, hearing, physical, speech, cognitive, and neurological disabilities.

Accessibility also benefits people in temporary or situational conditions, such as users with slow internet connections, broken devices, bright sunlight, or limited technical experience.
